# Content guidelines

## Authority and provenance

- Use the responsible national or regional government authority as the primary source for eligibility, fees, quotas, deadlines, processing rules, and application steps.
- Retain the exact source URL, review date, coverage level, and structured-field provenance in repository data.
- Record uncertainty explicitly. Never fill gaps from memory or infer a precise rule from promotional prose.
- Recheck time-sensitive facts before publication and when their review window expires.
- If an official source conflicts with a secondary source, use the official source and document unresolved ambiguity.

## Reader promises

- Clearly distinguish general educational information from personalized legal advice.
- Never predict approval or imply that a preliminary match means eligibility is confirmed.
- Explain `unknown` results and direct users to the responsible authority.
- Free pages must expose official links without payment or misleading pressure.
- Paid reports may organize verified public facts but may not invent facts, sources, or personalized legal conclusions.

## Coverage labels

- `deep`: reviewed program coverage for the declared scope.
- `overview`: principal pathways and official entry points only.
- `directory`: authority links only; no policy completeness claim.

Use the label visibly and consistently. A larger directory does not mean every country has matching rules or equal depth.

## Localization

- Write English as the default source experience and provide equivalent Chinese meaning under `/zh/`.
- Translate meaning, limitations, source context, and warnings—not just navigation labels.
- Do not silently add stronger eligibility claims in either locale.
- Keep proper program names and legal terms traceable to their official wording.
- Explicit user language selection overrides browser detection and is remembered locally.

## Style

- Prefer plain language, short sections, and scannable eligibility summaries.
- Put the official source and last-reviewed date near consequential claims.
- Avoid urgency, guaranteed-outcome language, artificial scarcity, and legal-advice framing.
